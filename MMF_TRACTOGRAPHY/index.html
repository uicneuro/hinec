    Moving frames tractography (algorithm: mmf)¶

    nim_tractography_mmf follows a fibre as a curve that carries its own orthonormal frame. Where the streamline trackers integrate the direction field dx/ds = v(x) and re-read v from the data at every stage, the moving-frames tracker advances the whole frame E = (e1; e2; e3) with the connection 1-forms of the data's frame field, evaluated on the direction of travel, and the position with the first frame vector:

    dx/ds = e1,        dE/ds = Omega E,        Omega_ij = w_ij(e1) = sum_k wij_k e1_k.

    Omega is skew-symmetric with the two curvature components w_12(e1), w_13(e1) and the twist w_23(e1). One step is the closed-form exponential of this motion (a rotation of the frame together with a circular arc of the position, by the Rodrigues formulas), so the frame stays exactly orthonormal without re-orthogonalisation and the step is exact for a planar arc of constant curvature, at any step size. Higher order comes from Lie group integrators built around that step: Lie–Euler, the exponential midpoint rule and the commutator-free fourth-order method CF4 (integrator.method: euler | rk2 | rk4). On a fibre with torsion the tracker drops the twist (it is not a datum of DTI, see below), so there Omega is not constant along the fibre in the carried frame and the step is exact only to the order of the integrator. The scheme itself is advanceFrame.m, shared with the moving-frames test suite (the Moving Frame Tractography/matlab folder), whose notes doc/MMFtractography_notes.tex give the mathematics, the gauge, the variants and the verification on analytic fields in two and three dimensions. This page covers only what connects the scheme to the voxel data of a nim.

    The frame field and its connection, from the voxels¶

    The DTI principal eigenvector is a line field: the sign the eigensolver writes for each voxel is arbitrary. The direction between voxels is therefore read from the interpolated dyadic v1 v1', which is sign-invariant — the same six interpolants, kernel (interpolation.method) and upsample as the hinec tracker — and the data's frame at a point x is

    e1(x)   = principal axis of D(x) = interp(v1 v1')
e2, e3  = a completion of e1 (reference-axis projection)
w_1j,k  = e_j . d e1 / dx_k      j = 2, 3     (the curvature components)
w_23,k  = 0                                    (the twist is not a datum of DTI)

    d e1/dx_k is a sign-aligned central difference of e1 of the interpolated field (mmf.gradient_delta voxels), so the connection is that of the field the streamline trackers integrate. With the spline kernel that field is C2 and the connection continuous; with the cubic and trilinear kernels the derivative of the field jumps at the voxel faces, the connection is discontinuous there, and the step — exact only for a connection that is constant along the arc — is then as accurate as any integrator on a kinked right-hand side (see the numbers below). config/mmf_dti.yml therefore uses the spline kernel. The scheme runs with 'Twist' 'zero' (a rotation-minimizing normal plane), so the arbitrary completion e2, e3 never enters the position: a step uses only the curvature vector (e1 . grad) e1 of the field, expressed in the carried frame through the gauge G = E Et' that advanceFrame computes at every evaluation. That gauge is also what absorbs the sign flips of the axial data — the "flip if the dot product is negative" of streamline tracking, extended to the whole frame.

    Modes (mmf.mode)¶

    mode what the carried frame is when
    transport carried by the connection only; the data enter through the curvature and the gauge the frame scheme proper; the heading, once in error, is never corrected
    reanchor replaced by the data frame after every step (signs chosen by the transported frame) the closest relative of streamline tracking, with an arc instead of a chord; the direction cannot drift from the data
    adaptive (default) transported, re-anchored only after a step at whose end the carried e1 has drifted from the data's e1 by mmf.reanchor_tol radians or more trusts the transported heading while it agrees with the data and snaps it back where it does not

    The drift at every point and the re-anchoring decisions are recorded (meta.mmf.reanchored_fraction; per step in meta.trace with debug.trace). The default tolerance 1e-3 rad is an order of magnitude below the heading error of a one-voxel CF4 step across a sharp bend and above the step-to-step wobble of interpolated data at normal steps (see the suite's notes, section on variants).

    Termination¶

    The connection is returned non-finite wherever a point is outside the volume, outside the propagation mask (or the brain mask), below the FA floor termination.fa_min or without a well-conditioned principal axis; advanceFrame stops a tract before a step at whose stages the connection is not finite. The stages are the start of a step, its midpoint (orders 2 and 4) and its end (order 4), so with orders 1 and 2 the scheme never examines the end of a step: the tracker drops a last node that lies outside tissue. Every node of a track is therefore a valid integration node inside tissue. Leaving the volume stops the tract as well, and the cut boundary point is dropped, so tracks consist of integration nodes only, like those of the other trackers.

    The angle budget termination.angle_max (degrees per voxel of arc, through nim_angle_limit: min(angle_max * h, 90) degrees per step, none for angle_max <= 0) becomes advanceFrame's MaxTurn. It bounds two turns: the rotation h |kappa| of a step, evaluated at every stage, and the turn of the heading as the data see it — the data's e1 at the end of a step against the heading at its start (modulo sign), in every mode. In the reanchor and adaptive modes that is the turn a re-anchoring would realise, the moving-frames counterpart of the angle between consecutive directions that the streamline trackers test, which could otherwise put a kink of up to 90 degrees into a track where the data's direction changes abruptly (a crossing seen in DTI, an interface between bundles); in transport mode, which never re-anchors, it stops a tract whose heading the data contradict beyond the budget instead of letting it run through them. Where the interpolated principal axis is piecewise constant with a swap plane, as across such an interface, the connection is zero except within gradient_delta of the plane and a step whose stages straddle it sees no curvature at all; this second test is what stops the tract there. Unlike the streamline trackers, whose sign-aligned turn cannot exceed 90 degrees, the 90 degree ceiling of nim_angle_limit is a real cap here. Note also that the curvature of the interpolated principal-axis field is large wherever the dyadic is nearly degenerate (crossings, planar regions: the derivative of the principal axis grows like 1/(lambda_1 - lambda_2)), so the budget stops more half-tracks in such regions than the node-to-node angle test of the streamline trackers does. termination.max_arc is advanceFrame's smax.

    The reasons are counted over the half-tracks of the seeds that were tracked in meta.termination (max_arc, angle, fa, outside_mask, outside, outside_domain, no_direction); an advanceFrame stop on a non-finite connection is resolved into one of these by probing the stages of the next step and their difference stencils. Seeds outside tissue or without a direction are not tracked and are counted by reason in meta.mmf.seeds_skipped. ACT masks are not used by this tracker.

    With debug.trace the per-step records of the traced seeds have the layout of the hinec tracker's (pos, dir = the carried e1, fa, h, chord, turn in degrees, flip always false, n_steps, termination), so nim_track_diagnose reads them, plus drift (angle to the data's e1), reanchored, kappa and s.

    What is not supported¶

    • field: csd. The connection handles of advanceFrame are functions of position alone, and choosing an FOD peak needs the incoming tangent. Use the hinec tracker for CSD.
    • integrator.method: rkf45. The exponential step has no embedded error estimate; the tracker rejects it (mmf:integrator).

    Verification¶

    tests/unit/TestMmfTracker.m runs the tracker on the rigid-rotation phantom of TestIntegratorOrder (circles of every radius, FA 1 in an annulus) from a seed on the circle of radius 15.5, for 60 voxels of arc in each direction with h = 0.25, and on the helix of tests/fixtures/make_synthetic_nim.m (radius 15, pitch 6, constant curvature and torsion) for 40 voxels each way:

    field kernel mode integrator max distance from the true curve (voxels)
    circle spline any euler / rk2 / rk4 1.1e-5 / 6e-6 / 5e-6
    circle cubic transport euler / rk2 / rk4 3.7e-3 / 7e-4 / 7e-4
    circle trilinear transport euler / rk2 / rk4 2.0e-2 / 1.5e-2 / 1.6e-2
    circle cubic / trilinear reanchor rk4 4e-5 / 1.4e-4
    circle trilinear (Euler template) 0.48
    helix spline adaptive euler / rk2 / rk4 1.0e-3 / 3.5e-4 / 5e-6

    On the circle the exponential step is exact, so with the spline kernel the residual is the interpolation error of the dyadic field and every mode and order gives the same 1e-5; the curvature returned with the tract is 1/15.508 to seven digits and nothing re-anchors at the default tolerance (the drift stays at 4e-8 rad). With the cubic and trilinear kernels the residual of the transport mode is dominated by the integration of the connection that jumps at the voxel faces, which is why the orders separate there and why re-anchoring to the data after every step (which follows the interpolated circle instead) is a hundred times better on trilinear data; the adaptive mode keeps the transported heading on these fields (no step drifts by 1e-3 rad) and coincides with transport. On the helix the twist is dropped and the residual is the integration error, with the orders 1, 2 and 4 cleanly separated; the curvature is recovered to 4e-8.

    The test also pins the tracker contract (nim_check_tracker_output, seed lattice and order, seed_density, random jitter, min_length, a run across the chunk boundary of 4000 seeds, a seed that cannot be tracked), the straight field (a line to roundoff), the sign alignment of the connection across the flip locus of the principal axis, the precedence of propagation_mask over the brain mask, the termination reasons (max_arc, angle, fa, outside_mask, outside) with every integrator, the angle budget on both the step and the data's heading (a 90-degree interface between bundles with the stages straddling the swap plane, and the crossing fixture at eight sub-voxel seed phases: every mode stops with angle and no track has a kink above the budget), the safety ceiling when the budget is disabled, the one-sided stencil at a volume face, a non-finite voxel in a spline field, the per-step trace and the rejection of rkf45 and csd.

    On the sample dataset (data/parcellation_sample/sample.mat, 128 x 128 x 50, config/mmf_dti.yml at seeding.density: 1, 147k seeds of which 100k lie in tissue) the tracker produces 69.7k tracks of at least 15 voxels in about 11 minutes, every node inside the mask and no node-to-node turn above the budget; hinec_dti.yml on the same seeds gives 74k in about 10 minutes, and for the 68.4k seeds both keep the two tracks agree to a median nearest-node distance of 0.25 voxel. The half-tracks end on the FA floor (77%), the volume faces (10%, the slab is cut at both ends), the angle budget (9%, against 0.5% for hinec, for the reason given above), the mask (3%) and a missing axis (1%).
